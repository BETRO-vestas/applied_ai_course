## The one idea to keep

A handover answers one question for the person taking over: *what do I need to
know so that nothing we promised gets dropped and nothing we decided gets
re-argued?*

Most handover notes are written from memory, in a hurry, in the last week on a
project. They capture what the outgoing person **thinks** matters. The gaps are
usually the informal items: a promise made in a chat, a date agreed in an email
thread, a decision reversed in a meeting that never reached the decision log.

## The Coral Ridge story

The outgoing connections lead wrote a short, accurate handover note to the
incoming compliance lead. It left out three things:

- a **model package date** committed to the customer by email (31 Mar 2026),
- a **weekly Friday update** promised to the customer in a Teams chat,
- an **open interface question** with the plant controller vendor, raised in
  meeting actions and never closed.

The incoming lead did not know about the first two. The model package arrived
three weeks late and the Friday updates stopped. The customer's complaint was the
first time the incoming lead heard of either commitment.

**Nothing was hidden. It was not retrieved.**

## Retrieval first, judgement second

A Copilot-assisted handover reverses the usual order:

1. **Retrieve:** Copilot searches mail, chats, meetings and files for decisions,
   commitments, open items, people and documents.
2. **Verify:** the outgoing lead checks the list, corrects it and adds what
   Copilot cannot know.
3. **Judge:** the outgoing lead adds the part only a person can give, such as
   "watch out for" and "the customer really cares about".
4. **Hand over:** a conversation, with the pack as the agenda.

Copilot does the remembering. People supply the meaning.

Copilot is weakest where the evidence is a phone call, a corridor conversation
or a tone. It also misses items that were never written down, so a retrieval
pass is a starting point, not a finished record.

## What would you do?

Your team's next handover is due in six weeks. Who writes it today, when does
it start, and how long does it take? Which part of that time is remembering and
which part is judgement?
