## The one idea to keep

A prompt that works is a team asset. Write it down as a template with placeholders, and check it before anyone relies on it.

## Good prompts are team assets

Once a prompt works, write it down. A tested prompt is a small piece of process: *"how we find commitments"* or *"how we prepare for a customer meeting"*. If it lives only in one person's chat history, it's lost like any other undocumented knowledge.

## Where to keep them

- **Saved prompts / prompt gallery:** the Copilot app lets you save prompts and reuse them. Depending on your organisation's settings, you may be able to share them with colleagues. Look for the prompt gallery or "saved prompts" in the app. Names change, so search Microsoft Support for the current location.
- **A team prompt page:** a simple Word page or Loop page in your team site, with one section per prompt: purpose, the prompt, an example output and known limits.
- **This app's Prompt Library:** copy-ready prompts from each module.

## Write prompts as templates

Use clear placeholders so anyone can reuse the prompt:

> *"List every commitment Northwind has made to **[CUSTOMER]** on **[PROJECT]** since **[DATE]**. Table: commitment, made by, to whom, date, due date, source. Newest first. 'Not stated' where a field is missing."*

## A simple quality bar for a team prompt

| Check | ✅ when |
|---|---|
| Purpose | One sentence says what it's for |
| Grounding | It names sources or scope |
| Checkability | It asks for citations |
| Gaps | It allows "not stated" or "not found" |
| Tested | Someone ran it on a real case and checked the output |
