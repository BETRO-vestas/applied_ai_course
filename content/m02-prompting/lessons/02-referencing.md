## The one idea to keep

Point Copilot at the exact file, person, meeting or email, and narrow the search with specific terms. Copilot then reads what you mean instead of guessing.

## Tell Copilot exactly what to read

In the Copilot app (Work mode) 🔒, Outlook, Teams and Word, type **`/`** to reference a specific **file, person, meeting or email**. This is the fastest way to ground a prompt, because Copilot reads what you point at instead of guessing.

| Reference | Example |
|---|---|
| A file | *"Using /CR-12 Hold point test plan…"* |
| A person | *"What has /Leo Martin asked us for in the last 3 months?"* |
| A meeting 🔒 | *"From /SouthGrid hold points meeting, list the actions for Northwind"* |
| An email 🔒 | *"Does /Re: VO-03 harmonic study change the decision in /CR-02?"* |

**Chat-only:** upload the files instead (paperclip), or open the document and use Copilot inside Word.

## Scope with words, too

When you can't reference something directly, narrow the search with words:

- **Time:** "since 1 April 2026", "in the last two weeks"
- **People:** "from Dana Whitfield or Ravi Patel"
- **Type:** "emails only", "meeting transcripts only"
- **Project terms:** the project name, the customer, a variation number (VO-03), a hold point (HP-2)

Specific terms are the best way to improve retrieval. **A variation number beats the words "the scope change".** This is one reason your team's naming habits matter (Module 7).

## Ask for the path, not just the answer

Add *"show me where you found each item"* or *"cite each line"*. Copilot returns references you can click. Opening two or three of them takes about 30 seconds, and it's the difference between using Copilot and trusting it blindly.
